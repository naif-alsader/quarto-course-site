The Story

The same content, three ways. Which one goes to the minister?

Three-minute recall first. Then the reveal.

THE REVEAL

Deck. Dashboard. Scrolly page. Three minutes each.

Fifteen minutes, and it is the whole opening. Same numbers, same data file, three vehicles. Do not editorialise while showing them.

Then the question, and wait for an answer: “which one do you send to the minister?”

Whatever they say, ask why. The reasons are the vehicle matrix, in their words, before you put it on a slide.

Choosing the vehicle

Explore or explain Audience Shelf life
Report Explain Anyone Months
Deck Explain A room, once One meeting
Dashboard Explore People with a question Until the data moves
Scroll view Explain Someone who missed the room Months
Scrolly Explain The public A campaign

Ten minutes. The column that decides is shelf life, not prettiness.

A dashboard answers questions the reader already knows to ask. If your reader does not yet know what to ask, a dashboard leaves them where they started — that line belongs in Block 3, not here.

Progressive disclosure

The line

One sentence. The reader who trusts you stops here.

The margin

Method, caveats, the denominator. The reader who doubts you.

The appendix

Everything. The reader who will replicate you.

Every vehicle needs all three. They differ in where the layers go.

Ten minutes. Draw the same three layers onto each of the four vehicles.

BLOCK 2 · 10:30–11:45

Presentations

A deck is a subset of a report

THE REVEAL

artifacts/revealjs-demo/ — Quarto’s own feature deck. Run it.

Five minutes, live, vendored locally. Code animation, auto-animate, a live ggplot, backgrounds, tabsets. Then open its source, which is shorter than anyone expects.

That is the reveal ritual, already built by someone else. Use it.

Your analysis is already the deck

format: revealjs
  • ## starts a slide. That is the whole model.
  • ::: {.columns} for side by side
  • . . . to pause
  • ::: {.notes} for speaker notes — you are reading mine right now

Twenty minutes: take your bulletin, add a format, cut it to five slides.

This is the thing everyone came for, so give it room. Open a Day 1 bulletin, change the format block, render. It is already a deck — a bad one. Then cut.

Cutting is the lesson. A deck is a subset of a report, not a different document.

Auto-animate

## {auto-animate=true}

The same element on two slides, moved — Reveal tweens between them.

Ten minutes. Show the “four moves” sequence: one code block, four states, animated. The technique demonstrated in the act of teaching it.

Keep it to one good use. A deck that animates everything is a deck nobody can read.

The five-slide manager briefing

1 · The number

2 · Why it moved

3 · What is at risk

4 · The choice

5 · The ask

Draft yours, against your own bulletin. Twenty minutes.

The ask is the slide people leave out, and it is the only one that makes a manager do something. “We recommend X by date Y” or “we need a decision on Z.”

Three volunteers present theirs. The room’s only question: would your manager act on it?

The presenter’s toolkit

Key What it does
s Speaker view — notes, timer, next slide
b Chalkboard — draw on the slide
g Jump to slide
o Overview
r Scroll view

Thirteen minutes, and it is the part they will use every week.

You have been using speaker view and the chalkboard for two days without explaining them. Say so now. Ask if anyone noticed.

Chalkboard is for the moment a manager asks “which quarter was that?” and you circle it. Jump-to-slide is for the Q&A when they ask about slide 12.

Scroll view

One .qmd. Press r and the deck becomes a scrollable page.

In the room

A deck.

On the intranet

A document, for the people who missed it.

It is also the offline-safe cousin of scrollytelling. Nothing to vendor.

Two minutes, but flag it clearly — this partly recovers what we cut. Closeread is beautiful and it needs a GitHub download. Scroll view is built in.

Getting it out

?print-pdf in the URL, then print to PDF.

Arabic decks: dir: rtl yes, rtl: true no — it breaks the export.

Five minutes. Demonstrate the export once. The RTL caveat is in their _quarto.yml already, with a comment — point at it.

Static

One HTML file. Email it. It opens.

A dashboard answers questions the reader already knows to ask. If they do not know what to ask, send a report.

Ten minutes. On CML, static is not a preference — it is the only thing that will actually reach a colleague.

This is where “dashboards explore, they rarely explain” belongs — as a caution inside the block that teaches them, not as the verdict before it.

Publishing responsibly

  • fig-alt on every figure — the caption is not alt text
  • Tables with real headers, not bold rows
  • Colour is never the only signal
  • Quarto 1.9 renders PDF/UA-tagged PDFs

Ten minutes. GASTAT publishes publicly, so this is an obligation rather than a nicety. UK Analysis Function guidance is the reference to hand them.

THE CLOSE · 10 MIN

  • Your data is on Hive. tools/extract.R is step zero.
  • quarto add will fail. Everything is vendored in _extensions/ and _fonts/.
  • renv::restore() from the internal mirror, not CRAN.
  • Ask the platform team for git.

Start with one report you already publish. Not a new one.

Ten minutes, and it is the most useful ten in the course.

The last line matters most: the failure mode is going home and starting a greenfield project that nobody needs. The win is taking one existing quarterly bulletin and making it re-render.

Ask each person to name, out loud, the one report they will convert. Out loud makes it real.
