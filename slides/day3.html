What a dashboard cannot do

A dashboard answers questions the reader already knows to ask.

They know the question

“How is my region doing?” — a dashboard is the right answer.

They do not

“Should we be worried about Jazan?” — send the bulletin. It makes the argument for them.

Seven minutes, and this is the block’s real content.

Dashboards explore; they rarely explain. A minister does not arrive with a filter in mind — they arrive wanting to know whether to act.

Tie it back to Block 1: this is the shelf-life column in another form. Build the dashboard for the analyst who visits weekly, write the bulletin for the minister who reads once.

Publishing responsibly

Static and self-contained means it travels. Make sure it arrives readable.

  • fig-alt on every figure — the caption is not alt text
  • Tables with real headers, not bold rows
  • Colour is never the only signal
  • Quarto 1.9 renders PDF/UA-tagged PDFs

Ten minutes. The bridge is the previous slide: you have just made a file that anyone can open and forward. That is exactly why alt text and real table headers matter — it will reach a screen reader you never met.

GASTAT publishes publicly, so this is an obligation rather than a nicety. UK Analysis Function guidance is the reference to hand them.
